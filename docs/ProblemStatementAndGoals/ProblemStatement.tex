\subsection{Problem}

Paediatric ophthalmology appointments can be stressful and unfamiliar for children.
Procedures such as vision testing, eye drops, and slit-lamp examinations may cause anxiety, particularly when children do not know what to expect.
This anxiety can make it difficult to cooperate during examinations, potentially affecting the quality and completeness of clinical assessments.
For example, a study of 298 children undergoing cycloplegic eye examinations found that 26\% experienced significant distress before receiving eye drops,
while uncooperative children were also more anxious and required more time to complete the examination \cite{ChiaEtAl2015}.

Children and their families therefore need adequate preparation for common ophthalmology procedures before attending an appointment.
Without appropriate preparation, unfamiliar clinical experiences may contribute to increased anxiety, reduced cooperation, and longer or more difficult appointments.
This project addresses the need for an accessible, child-friendly way to help children become more familiar and comfortable with the paediatric ophthalmology experience.

\subsection{Mohammad Bilal}

\textbf{What went well while writing this deliverable?}

One thing that went well was that the problem itself was relatively clear to the team.
The project description gave a good starting point for identifying the main issue and
explaining why it matters. The team was also able to connect the problem to the clinical
context and identify the main users and stakeholders involved. Looking into existing research
also helped support the problem statement with evidence rather than relying only on assumptions
about children's experiences during ophthalmology appointments. Meeting with the supervisor was
also helpful in clarifying the project's scope and better understanding the overall vision for the project.

\textbf{What pain points did you experience during this deliverable, and how did you resolve them?}

One of the main challenges was deciding how much detail to include without making the problem
statement too focused on the implementation. It was easy to start describing specific app features
instead of focusing on the actual problem being addressed. The team resolved this by going back to
the purpose of the deliverable and keeping the problem statement focused on the clinical experience,
the needs of the users, and the overall problem. Another challenge was making sure the goals were
specific enough to be meaningful while still leaving room for the implementation to change as the project develops.
